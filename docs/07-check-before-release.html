Check Before Release

A short review that catches almost every embarrassing mistake

In this chapter

  • Find leftover template text and placeholder settings
  • Review a course for accessibility in ten minutes
  • Test the course on a small screen and in dark mode
  • Recognise the legal and privacy questions to settle before going public
  • Get one other person to test it

Once a course is public, learners will find every typo, broken link and leftover “Placeholder” for you. A structured check before release costs an hour and saves the awkward emails. Work through the sections below in order.

1 1. Content

  1. Every chapter has objectives at the top, and every objective is covered by at least one question.
  2. Read each chapter once, aloud or slowly. You will hear what you cannot see: sentences that are too long, steps out of order.
  3. Run the spell checker. In RStudio, open a chapter and press F7 (or use Edit → Check Spelling). Do it for every file.
  4. Check facts, numbers and dates, especially anything that might have changed since you wrote it.
  5. Click every link to an outside website. Web addresses go out of date.

2 2. Leftover template text

The commonest slip is publishing the template’s own words. Use RStudio’s search across all files: Edit → Find in Files… (Ctrl+Shift+F, or Cmd+Shift+F on a Mac). Search for each of these, and fix every hit outside the parts of the course that you deliberately kept:

  • Course Template
  • example.org
  • Placeholder
  • Your name
  • placeholder.svg

Then open _quarto.yml and check the lines marked CHANGE: the course title, the description, the web address (site-url), the chapter list and the footer. Also check index.qmd: the author line and the date.

3 3. The look

Render the whole website with Render Website, not just a single file, so no stale pages remain. Then look at it the way learners will:

  • On a small screen. Make the browser window narrow (drag its edge) until the layout switches to the phone view. Text should stay readable, and no horizontal scrolling should be needed. Better still, open the published site on a real phone once.
  • In dark mode. Switch your computer to dark appearance and reload. Text, boxes and the logo should stay legible.
  • In the print preview, if learners will print chapters.
  • In the search. Type a word you know is in a chapter, and confirm it is found. Search works on the published site, and may not when you open the files from disk.

4 4. Accessibility

A course that not everyone can use is unfinished. Ten minutes:

  1. Alt text on every image (Chapter 4). No “image1”.
  2. Headings in order, never skipped, never faked with bold text (Chapter 3).
  3. Link text that makes sense alone. Search your chapters for “click here”.
  4. No information by colour alone. If a sentence says “the red button”, add a name.
  5. Video has captions or a transcript.
  6. Try the keyboard. Press Tab repeatedly through a page and check that you can reach and use links, menus and the quiz buttons without a mouse.

The standard behind these points is the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (World Wide Web Consortium 2023); the template’s colours already pass its contrast requirements, so you only need to keep to the habits above.

5 5. Legal and privacy

Ask, do not guess

The rules for what a public university website must contain, and what may be published, are set by your institution. Confirm the following with the responsible office before release.

  • Imprint and privacy notice. Public websites in Germany normally need an imprint (Impressum) and, where personal data is processed, a privacy notice. Ask your communications or data-protection office for the standard wording and the address to link to. The footer in _quarto.yml has a commented-out example showing where such a link goes.
  • Picture, text and video rights. Do you have the right to publish every image, table and quotation? Is each one credited as its licence requires?
  • Patient and personal data. Nothing that identifies a patient or a colleague, in text, images, screenshots or file names. Look at screenshots for name fields, record numbers and dates of birth.
  • Third-party services. If you have embedded a video or map from an outside provider, your data-protection office needs to have approved it.
  • The licence of your own material. State how others may reuse the course, if at all, in the footer or on the landing page.

6 6. A fresh pair of eyes

Ask one person who does not know the subject to work through one chapter while you watch, and say nothing. Note every place they hesitate, re-read or click the wrong thing. These are the places to improve. You will learn more in twenty minutes than in a day of rereading your own text.

7 7. Before you press publish

  • The whole website has been rendered again after your last change.
  • Nothing is left in the sidebar that you do not want learners to see. Drafts start with an underscore.
  • You know what web address the course will have, and it is entered as site-url.

You are ready for Chapter 8.

8 Check your understanding

Which of these is the best way to find leftover placeholder text across the whole project?

A search across all files finds every hit in seconds, including ones in files you have not opened for weeks. Reading is slower and easily misses things.

A screenshot in your chapter shows the RStudio window on your own computer, including a project name and your user name. What should you do?

Screenshots regularly reveal names, file paths and records that their authors did not notice. Look at the whole picture, at full size, before publishing.

Why render the whole website before release, rather than only the chapter you last changed?

A full render leaves the output folder in the exact state that will be published, with no stale leftovers from earlier versions.

The menu you use in RStudio to search through every file at once is called Edit → Find in ?.
